# Philosophy

Kaizen exists to help me answer five questions, every day, with as little friction as possible.

1. **What am I trying to accomplish this year?**
2. **What should I be focusing on this week?**
3. **What should I be doing today?**
4. **Where is my time actually going?**
5. **Am I making progress, and what can I improve tomorrow?**

The five questions form one loop: the year shapes the week, the week shapes today, today's time shows the truth,
and reflection feeds back into tomorrow, the next week and the year. Kaizen is that loop, made easy.

## Three philosophies, one job each

| Source | Its job in Kaizen | Ideas I rely on (in my own words) |
|---|---|---|
| **The 7 Habits of Highly Effective People** (Stephen Covey) | **Direction** — principles, values and priorities: deciding what deserves my attention. | Start from the end I want. Put important-but-not-urgent work first. Plan the week before the day. Focus on what I can influence. Make time to rest and renew. |
| **Atomic Habits** (James Clear) | **Mechanics** — how behaviour actually changes. | Systems beat goals. Tiny habits compound. Make the good habit obvious, attractive, easy and satisfying. Every habit has a minimum version. Stack new habits onto existing ones. Never miss twice. Habits shape who I become. |
| **Kaizen** (continuous improvement) | **Practice** — the daily loop of getting a little better. | Plan, do, check, adjust. Look at what really happened, not what I hoped. Find the waste and the friction. Change one small thing at a time. Keep what works. |

Covey decides *what* matters, Atomic Habits makes doing it *automatic*, Kaizen makes it *better every day*.

## Principles for every build decision

1. **Every feature must answer one of the five questions.** If it doesn't, it goes to the backlog or nowhere.
2. **Close the loop.** Features are most valuable where they connect questions: a goal shows up in the week, the
   week shows up today, time is tagged to goals, reflection turns into one action for tomorrow.
3. **Consistency over perfection.** Show the minimum version as a win. Streaks forgive a single miss.
   Never shame, never gamify for its own sake.
4. **Make the right thing the easy thing.** The daily actions — check a habit, start a timer, write a few lines —
   must each take seconds and few taps, one-handed, on the iPhone.
5. **Honest data.** Show where time actually went next to where I meant it to go. The gap is the lesson.
6. **Reflection must produce an action.** Every review ends in one small, concrete improvement.
7. **Small, steady improvements to the app itself.** Build in small steps, use it daily, fix the friction I
   actually feel before adding features. Six exceptional features beat fifteen mediocre ones.
8. **Calm and private.** The app should feel peaceful and keep my data on my device. Prompts are original
   wording inspired by these ideas — never passages from the books.

## How to use this file

Before starting a phase or feature, check it against the five questions and the eight principles.
When a design choice is unclear, choose the option that makes the loop tighter and the daily action easier.
